The problem

A missed call doesn't wait for you.

When someone's water heater goes at 8pm, they don't leave a voicemail and sit tight. They hang up and call the next company on the list. That call is worth the same as any other — you just weren't there to take it.

And it's worst exactly when you're doing well. The busier the week, the more of the phone gets missed: you're on a job, your front desk is already on another call, or it's simply gone six. The calls you lose are the ones your advertising just paid for.

What it does

It answers, it asks the right things, and it tells you.

It's an AI assistant on the phone, set up on your business — your hours, your service area, the jobs you take and the ones you don't, answered in words you sign off on before it goes live.

Answers the everyday questions

Are you open Saturday? Do you come out to Cedar Park? Do you work on that brand? It answers from what you told us about your business — and when it doesn't know, it says so and takes a message instead of guessing.

Books the job

It offers real slots from your calendar and puts the appointment in, with the caller's number attached. Someone ringing to ask when you're coming gets told, without anyone having to look it up.

Passes emergencies to a person

Gas smell, water coming through a ceiling, no heat in a freeze — those don't get a message taken. They go straight through to whoever is on call, with a quick handover so they're not starting from nothing. You set what counts as urgent.

Gets the details you actually need

Name, callback number, the address, what's wrong, how soon they need you, and whether they're already a customer. The things that decide whether a job is worth driving to — captured the same way every time.

Texts or emails you a summary

After every call, a short write-up of who rang and what they wanted. You can read it between jobs and decide what needs you tonight and what can wait until the morning.

English and Spanish

If your area needs it, callers get handled in either language rather than hanging up. Optional — we turn it on if it's useful where you work.

How it works

Four steps, and none of them are your job.

Step 1 · Talk

A 15-minute call

How many calls you get, when you miss them, who's on call, what counts as an emergency in your trade. If it isn't worth doing for you, we'll say so on that call.

Step 2 · Build

We set it up on your business

Your hours, your area, what you will and won't quote over the phone, your on-call numbers. Then we test it hard — including the awkward calls — and you listen to it before anything goes live.

Step 3 · Switch on

You forward the calls you'd miss

Your phone rings first, exactly as it does now. Only what goes unanswered — or comes in after hours — rolls over. Your number doesn't change, and it's reversible in a minute.

Step 4 · Tune

We watch it and report

For the first two weeks we read every call. After that you get a monthly rundown: what came in, what got booked, what got passed to a person.

Straight answers

What we'll tell your customers, and what we won't do.

It's your name on the phone. We'd rather you knew exactly how this behaves before you put your reputation behind it.

  • Callers are told. It says it's an assistant and that the call is recorded, in the greeting, before anything else. We don't pretend it's a person, and we won't set it up to.
  • "Let me speak to someone" works. Ask for a person and you get put through, first time, without having to argue with it.
  • Urgent calls go to a human. You define what urgent means in your trade. Those get transferred, not summarised and sent on.
  • It never takes a card number. Not over the phone, not ever. If someone tries to pay, it takes a message for you.
  • It only calls people back, never out. No cold calling, no outbound dialling. It answers your phone, and that's all.
  • Your customers are your customers. The call records are yours, we keep them only as long as you want them kept, and we don't sell or share them with anybody.

Where it's genuinely not the right answer

Some people hang up the moment they realise it isn't a person — not many, but it happens, and anyone who tells you it never does is selling. That's exactly why we start at after-hours: those calls were going to voicemail anyway, so the worst case is where you already were.

Doctors' and dentists' offices are a different job, with rules around patient information that this setup isn't built for. If that's you, say so on the call and we'll point you at someone who does it properly rather than take the work.

Questions people ask

The five we get every time.

Does this replace my receptionist?

No, and we'd talk you out of using it that way. Your front desk knows your customers, reads a room and handles the ones that need handling. What they can't do is be there at 9pm, or be on two calls at once.

This picks up what falls off the edges — after hours, at lunch, when the line is already busy — so nothing lands in voicemail. Most people find it makes the front desk's day quieter, not shorter.

Can callers still reach a real person?

Yes, and it's the first thing we test. Ask for a person and you get transferred straight away — you don't have to say it three times or wait for it to finish a sentence.

Anything you've told us counts as an emergency skips the conversation entirely and rings your on-call number, whether the caller asks or not.

Do I keep my number?

Yes. Your number stays exactly where it is, with the same carrier. Nothing gets ported and nothing on your van, your truck or your Google listing has to change.

All that changes is a forwarding setting on your line: calls you don't answer, or calls outside your hours, roll over to us instead of to voicemail. You can switch it off yourself in about a minute.

Does it work with the software I already use?

Google Calendar and Cal.com it books into directly. Several common CRMs connect too, and we'll tell you on the first call whether yours is one of them — before you've paid for anything.

Some field-service systems need custom work, and a few don't let anyone in at all. We'll say which yours is straight, rather than promising an integration and discovering the problem later. Worst case it still answers, books into a calendar and emails you the details — you just copy it across.

How long does setup take?

Usually one to two weeks from the kickoff call. Most of that is us building it on your business and testing it, not you doing paperwork — you're looking at about an hour of your time in total.

Nothing goes live until you've listened to it handle your own awkward calls and told us you're happy.
