Where the clicks go

An audit of 73 Texas home-service businesses currently paying for Meta ads.

Published by ForgeLabs AI  ·  Fieldwork August–September 2026  ·  Public sources only, inspection only

The finding
Nearly three in five were paying for clicks that never reached a page they own.

Not a page that converts badly — no page at all. The click ended in a chat inbox, on a social profile, or nowhere. Every one of these businesses had ads running live on the day we looked.

Worth being fair about

Why a Messenger destination is not automatically a mistake.

Click-to-Message is a legitimate Meta objective and for some businesses it is the right one. We do not count it as a broken funnel. Two things are worth knowing about it anyway.

Withdrawn: the sign-in wall count

This panel carried a count of Messenger destinations that present a sign-in wall when logged out. It has been withdrawn. An fb.me destination is not necessarily a chat thread — paired with a "Get quote" button it is an Instant Form, which works as designed. Reclassifying on the button moved 70 rows out of the Messenger bucket, so the count is not sound and is not being restated until the re-audit finishes.

There is nothing to measure

No owned page means no conversion event, no cost per lead, and no audience to rebuild later. The spend produces conversations that live inside someone else's platform and disappear from your reporting.

The sharper problem is a mismatch: an ad whose button says Get a quote leading to an unmonitored chat thread is promising a process the destination cannot deliver.

How this was done

Public sources only, inspection only.

Everything here came from the Meta Ad Library and from loading pages the way a customer would.

Step 1 · Find

Active ads only

Businesses were found by searching the Meta Ad Library for active ads by trade and metro. Only ads running at the time of the audit were included.

Step 2 · Follow

To the true endpoint

Every click path was followed all the way through link shorteners, tracking redirects, and landing-page redirects.

Step 3 · Inspect

Does it actually work

Does it load, is a form wired to send anywhere, does a booking widget render, is there a call-to-action visible at a phone-sized screen, is a tracking pixel present.

Step 4 · Record

Against the specific ad

Each finding was recorded against the ad it came from, so any individual result can be re-checked.

Where this method was wrong, and what changed

Two early findings were false. The Ad Library results page lazy-renders each ad's link card, so an ad whose destination had not loaded looked identical to one with no destination — that produced two bogus "goes nowhere" results, both caught by hand. A third came from reading links across a permalink page that renders several ads at once, attributing a sibling ad's landing page to the wrong ad.

Both are now method rules: destinations are only ever read from an ad's own permalink, scoped to its own Library ID block. The figures above were re-derived afterwards. We would rather publish the correction than the cleaner story.

What we deliberately did not do

No forms submitted, no appointments booked, no messages sent, no calls made. No business was contacted to produce this, and no private, purchased or scraped personal data was used.

What we are not publishing, and why
No business is named in this report, and none will be.

Naming them — or publishing the ad identifiers that would let anyone look them up — would mean labelling real local businesses as having a marketing problem, in a document written by the company that wants to sell them the fix.

So this report gives percentages and patterns only. Trade categories with fewer than five businesses in the sample are folded into the totals rather than broken out, because a category of two is a category that identifies its members.

Business owners who want to know what their own funnel does can ask us. That answer belongs to them, not to a public document.

  • 73 home-service businesses, all with Meta ads running live during the audit window of August–September 2026.
  • Geography: Texas metros, predominantly greater Austin, with a smaller number from Dallas and Houston. This is an Austin-weighted sample and should not be read as representative of Texas as a whole.
  • Not a random sample. These businesses were found by searching for active ads in specific trades. They represent businesses that advertise on Meta, not home-service businesses generally.
  • A point-in-time snapshot. Ad campaigns change weekly. A business that had no destination in September may have one now.
  • Absence is harder to prove than presence. Where a check could not see something, we recorded it as unverified rather than as a defect. Findings that depended on a method we could not confirm were excluded from these totals entirely.
  • What we could not measure: whether anyone was actually answering the Messenger inboxes, what any of these businesses pay per lead, and whether their ads were profitable regardless. Those answers require access we did not have and did not ask for.
What it means if you are running ads

Three questions worth answering about your own funnel. None of them require us.

One

Open your own ad on a phone, signed out of Facebook

Where does it land? That is what a stranger sees — and a stranger is who you are paying to reach.

Two

If it lands on a page: is there a pixel on it?

If not, every optimisation decision you or your agency makes is being made blind.

Three

If it lands in a chat: who reads that inbox at 8pm on a Saturday?

That is when a homeowner notices the AC has failed and starts working down the list.
