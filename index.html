Our rule

Ads that open a chat box

Plenty of ads open a Facebook message instead of a web page. We tried it ourselves: if the person isn't already logged into Facebook, they get a login screen instead of you. Most of them give up there.

No idea what's working

Over half of the ones with a real page had no tracking on it. So there's no way to know which ads actually bring in jobs and which ones just burn money — including for whoever they're paying to run them.

The ad promises what the page can't do

The button says Get a quote. It opens a chat nobody's watching. That person was ready to book you right then — and there was nobody there to take it.

Being straight about what this is

Most of these businesses are around Austin, so it's not a picture of all of Texas. We found them by searching for ads that were running, which means they're businesses that advertise — not every home service business. And it's one day's snapshot; ads change every week. We also couldn't tell whether anyone was reading those chat inboxes, or whether the ads were making money anyway. That would need access to their accounts, which we didn't have and didn't ask for.

We don't name any of them, and we won't. Publicly pointing at a local business and saying their ads are broken — in a document written by the people who'd like to sell them the fix — isn't something we're willing to do. So you get the numbers and the patterns, nothing that identifies anyone.
