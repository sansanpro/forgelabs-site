By design

A chat thread isn't automatically wrong

Click-to-Message is a legitimate objective. But we verified by hand that opening one without a Facebook session shows a sign-in wall, not a conversation — and on desktop that's a lot of people. We recorded 25 instances of the pattern.

No pixel means no answers

More than half the businesses that had built a landing page couldn't tell which ad produced a job. Every optimisation decision after that is made blind — including the ones an agency bills for.

The mismatch costs the most

An ad whose button says Get a quote leading to an unmonitored inbox is promising a process the destination can't deliver. The visitor came ready. Nothing was there to catch them.

About this report — read this part

The sample is Austin-weighted and should not be read as representative of Texas as a whole. It is not a random sample — these businesses were found by searching for active ads in specific trades, so they represent businesses that advertise on Meta, not home-service businesses generally. It is a point-in-time snapshot; campaigns change weekly. We could not measure whether anyone was actually reading those inboxes, what any of them pay per lead, or whether the ads were profitable anyway — that needs access we didn't have and didn't ask for.

No business is named in the report, and none will be. Publicly labelling named local businesses as having a marketing problem — in a document written by the company that wants to sell them the fix — isn't a trade we're willing to make. So the report gives percentages and patterns only. Trades with fewer than five businesses in the sample are folded into the totals, because a category of two identifies its members.
